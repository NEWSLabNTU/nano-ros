An image that keeps its own `main` (no launch file) and turns on `param_services` or `lifecycle` in `system.toml` now serves every runtime service on every node. Before, its sizing took none of those families into account: a two-node zenoh image lost the second node's last parameter service from the ROS graph (`ros2 node info` listed five of six), and an XRCE image could not create even the first node's parameter services. `nros build` now writes a sizing descriptor for such an image stating its service families, and a build that cannot count the image's nodes sizes the per-node services generously instead of for one node. A hosted zenoh image that states nothing at all now reserves room for the runtime's service families in its liveliness pool, as its queryable table already did.
