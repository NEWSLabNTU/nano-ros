Ten shipped crates now carry `#![forbid(unsafe_code)]`, where before not one crate in `packages/{core,api,platform,boards,rmw,drivers,tooling}` denied or forbade it — so `nros-serdes` (the CDR serializer, ~5 000 LOC, the crate the Kani/Verus harnesses assume) was held at zero unsafe by nothing but habit. Re-measured before acting: thirteen crates are at literal zero, not the three the issue found, and the ten that took the lint are the ones whose ROLE implies the property — three pure data-transformation core crates, five host-side build-script helper libraries, two board crates that carry no hardware access. Three were deliberately left alone: two FreeRTOS board crates whose own docs say a Rust console writer is still to come, and a `-sys` crate that is thirteen lines of doc comment. `forbid` is the gate rather than a census script for this population: it cannot be locally overridden (it outranks a later `allow`), it fails in the author's own `cargo check`, it has no authored crate list to drift out of step with the rule, and it cannot be satisfied by a spelling change. Each one's enforcement was MEASURED with a planted `unsafe` block, ten of ten; `just check no-std` plus explicit `thumbv7m-none-eabi` / `riscv32imc-unknown-none-elf` checks cover the bare-metal half.
