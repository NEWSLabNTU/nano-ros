A hazard's rung route is monitored on the image. play_launch now writes each rung of a hazard's ladder into the SystemModel with the route its checker charged (from the detection to the rung's first output), and the hazard observer judges the rung actually running: the top rung, or the lower rung whose mode the executor is already in or falls to while the fault holds, timed from that rung's `starts_at` detection (or from the fall). A rung that answers late, or not at all within its route, is reported as `hazard-route-runtime`, on the Rust `nros::main!` road and both C/C++ entry shapes. The reaction hook's event gains `fired_at_us`, the executor's monotonic microseconds when the verdict fired, so a hook can time its own reaction from the detection. `NROS_CPP_HAZARD_ROW_STORAGE` grows to 112 bytes on a 64-bit host and 80 on a 32-bit target, and each rung takes `NROS_CPP_HAZARD_RUNG_STORAGE` (32 / 24).
